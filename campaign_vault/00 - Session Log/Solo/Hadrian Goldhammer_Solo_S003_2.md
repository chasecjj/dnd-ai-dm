## Solo Adventure Log

### Turn 0
**Player:** [Session Start]
**DM:** The memory of the lie still tastes sweet on your tongue. In a different alley, not so long ago, the gas lamps had sputtered and died at the perfect moment. You had watched the thug, Grigor, shrink under the weight of your words—a test from his unseen boss, a trial he was failing. The fear in his eyes was a far greater reward than any coin. Power, you had learned early, was not about the strength in your arm, but the conviction in your voice.

Now, some time later, you find yourself perched in the familiar shadows of the Dock Ward. You sit on the rain-slicked tiles of a warehouse roof, the salty spray of the Sea of Swords misting your face. Below, the world is a chaotic tapestry of sound and smell. The groan of ship timbers straining against their moorings, the raucous laughter spilling from the tavern known as The Drowned Rat, the rhythmic crash of waves against the pier—it's the lullaby of the city's grimiest corner. The air is thick with the scent of brine, tar, and rotting fish, a perfume you've known your entire life. From your vantage point, the flickering lanterns of the docks look like captured stars, illuminating the scurrying figures below.

Your eyes, accustomed to the gloom, sweep the scene. You spot a clandestine exchange on the furthest pier, two cloaked figures trading a heavy coffer under the barest sliver of moonlight. Near the entrance to The Drowned Rat, a small crowd is gathered around a dice game, their purses jingling with promise. But it’s a scene in the narrow alley directly below that snags your attention. Two broad-shouldered toughs have a well-dressed merchant pinned against a wall of damp brick. Their voices are too low to hear, but their posture is universal.

One of the thugs shoves the merchant hard, and the man stumbles, his plea a faint squeak. The second thug, the one with a shock of red hair visible even in the dim light, turns his head just so. A jolt, sharp and unwelcome, runs through you. You know that stance. You know that profile. It's Finn—the same Finn who taught you how to palm a coin purse and which rooftops were safest to sleep on. He draws a short, wicked-looking blade, its edge glinting in the lantern light as he presses it to the merchant's throat.

### Turn 1
**Player:** Not one to interrupt a thief at work, I slip down from my perch and circle around the group to just get close enough to hear what Finn is up to and what game he is playing.

For the DM: Can you help me remember what I know about Finn?
**DM:** You slide from the rain-slicked crate with the practiced silence of a cat. Your boots make barely a whisper on the grimy cobblestones, a sound swallowed instantly by the thick, cloying fog that rolls in off the harbor. The damp air clings to your leather armor as you hug the shadows of stacked barrels and forgotten fishing nets, circling the small knot of figures huddled under a sputtering gas lamp.

The sight of Finn at work stirs something in you, a memory sharp as a shard of glass. You see his hands, years younger, guiding your own clumsy fingers, teaching you the delicate art of lifting a coin purse, the precise pressure needed. He taught you how to read the alleys, how to find shelter, how to turn a sob story into a hot meal. He was your mentor in this grimy, unforgiving world. Watching him now, a predator circling his mark, you feel a familiar, complicated mix of respect and something sharper.

You settle behind a stack of waterlogged crates, close enough now that their voices cut through the muffled sounds of the docks. Finn has his arm around the shoulders of a portly merchant, a man whose fine clothes are hopelessly out of place here. The merchant clutches a small, velvet-wrapped box to his chest, his eyes wide and fearful.

"It's a simple exchange, Master Borin," Finn's voice is a low, reasonable purr—the same one he used to use to calm a spooked mark. "The 'Eye of the Deep' for your safe passage. My employers... they don't like complications."

The merchant, Borin, stammers, "But-but I was told the delivery was for tomorrow! At the Gilded Griffin! This... this is highly irregular."

Finn just smiles, a flash of teeth in the gloom. He hasn't seen you. From this vantage point, you have a clear view of the exchange, hidden by shadow and fog.

### Turn 2
**Player:** Do I know who Finn generally works for? Or is he an opportunist like me?
**DM:** The knowledge surfaces from the grimy recesses of your memory, pieced together from whispers in taverns and truths learned on the street. Finn was never a joiner. He was a jackal, not a wolf—too proud for a pack, always nipping at the heels of larger predators but never pledging allegiance. He taught you to be the same. He works for coin, for opportunity, for himself. That's why hearing him tangled up with the Xanathar Guild feels so wrong, like watching a stray dog wander into a dragon's lair. He's not one of them; he's in their debt. And they are not known for their forgiveness.

Your thoughts are confirmed as Finn practically pleads with the merchant, his usual swagger replaced by a frantic, desperate energy. Then, a new shape detaches itself from the oppressive fog. Heavy, deliberate footsteps echo on the wet cobblestones, and a hulking figure emerges into the sputtering lamplight. It’s a bugbear, its matted fur smelling of stale ale and wet fur, a dented shield strapped to its arm. On the shield, a crudely painted symbol: a yellow eye with ten stalks.

The bugbear ignores Master Borin entirely, its small, dark eyes fixing on your old mentor. A low growl rumbles in its chest. "Time's up, little man," it rasps, its voice like stones grinding together. "The Boss wants his delivery." Finn freezes, his face a pale mask of terror in the dim light. The merchant takes a fearful step back, raising his hands as if to ward off the violence hanging thick in the air. The bugbear's shadow swallows Finn whole.

From the shadows, you have a clear view of the unfolding disaster. What do you do?

### Turn 3
**Player:** I have to help my old mentor, but I need to be smart. I’m not as strong as someone like Victor. Using the fog and darkness as my cover, I climb up the side of a warehouse overlooking the bug bear and others. While I climb I try to figure out any advantage I might have over the Bugbear.
**DM:** The salt-crusted wood of the warehouse is slick with mist, offering little purchase, but your fingers find the weathered seams and you haul yourself upward. You move with the quiet confidence of a rooftop cat, your ascent a silent crawl into the clinging grey fog. Below, the bugbear's guttural threats and Finn's strained replies become muffled, distorted by the damp air.

From the peak of the slanted roof, you flatten yourself against the cold tiles. The scene is laid out beneath you, a tense little drama playing out in a single pool of sputtering gaslight. The bugbear's hulking form is pure menace, its shadow swallowing both Finn and the trembling merchant. Your mentor looks cornered, his usual swagger replaced by a rigid tension in his shoulders.

Your rogue's mind, always seeking the advantage, finds one almost immediately. A heavy loading crane is bolted to the eaves just a few feet from you. From it, a thick rope holds a massive, net-wrapped crate of salted fish dangling twenty feet over the alley. It sways gently in the harbor breeze, directly over the bugbear's head. The release lever is right there, a tempting bar of rusted iron within your reach.

Just as the plan begins to form, a faint scraping sound cuts through the night. It's not from the docks below. It comes from across the alley, on the roof of the opposite warehouse. You catch a brief, sharp glint—moonlight on a crossbow bolt, or perhaps a spyglass lens. You are not the only one watching this from the shadows.

Abandoning the effort, you slide on your belly across the slick tiles toward the rusted iron lever of a rooftop crane. You move with what you believe is absolute silence, your fingers finding purchase on the cold metal. You try to ease the heavy arm of the lever into a new position, using it as a shield between you and the unseen watcher. But the mechanism has been left to the salt and the rain for too long.

A deafening, grinding *SCREEECH* of protesting metal tears through the damp air, echoing across the docks.

Down below, all motion ceases. The bugbear's head snaps up, its piggish eyes scanning the rooftops with sudden, violent interest. Finn freezes, his hand hovering over the lockbox, his face a mask of alarm. Even from here, you can feel the unseen sniper's attention swing directly to your position. Your cover is blown. Your hand rests on the lever, ready to pull, but the element of surprise is gone, shattered by a single, clumsy noise.

### Turn 6
**Player:** I pull the lever and leap onto the now falling platform, careful to keep it between me and any watchful eyes. I don’t want the individual with the spyglass to know I was there or what happened
**DM:** With a grunt, you heave the rusted lever. It shrieks in protest, a sound of metal grinding against metal that seems far too loud in the damp quiet. A heavy *clank* echoes as the locking pin gives way, and you throw yourself forward, leaping into the space the platform just occupied.

Your landing is clumsy, a jarring thud rather than a cat's silent tread. Your boots skid on the slick wood, and you flail for a moment, catching your balance just as the entire structure lurches and drops from beneath the rooftop. Ropes groan and pulleys scream as you plummet, the foggy air rushing past you. You press yourself flat, letting the platform's wooden frame shield you from any eyes above. To the distant watcher, it would seem as though a piece of old machinery simply, finally, gave way to time and rot.

The platform smashes into the cobblestones of the alley below with a tremendous, splintering crash. The sound rips through the fog. Instantly, the bugbear and his cronies spin around, weapons half-raised, their attention fixed on the wreckage. Finn, his argument cut short, stares at the splintered wood with wide, startled eyes.

You are on the ground now, hidden in the sudden chaos. Their backs are to you, their focus entirely on the noise you created. The perfect distraction. What do you do?
